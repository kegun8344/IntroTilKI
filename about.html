How this app was made

Movie Night Matcher is a student project for DAVE3625 (Introduction to AI) at OsloMet. It was built by "vibe coding": describing what I wanted in plain language and letting an AI assistant write, test and fix the code.

The problem

Choosing a movie together takes longer than watching one. Streaming apps show you endless rows, but they don't help a group agree. This app borrows the swipe-to-match idea from dating apps: everyone swipes the same stack in private, and only the movies everyone said yes to come out the other end.

How to play

  1. Add 2 to 6 people, pick a vibe and any filters, and choose how many movies to swipe.
  2. Swipe right for yes, left to skip, and up for must watch (3 per person, so they mean something). Tap a ticket to flip it for the director, cast, trailer and where to stream.
  3. Pass the phone, or send a link so each person swipes on their own phone. The whole game is stored in the link, so there is no server and no account.
  4. See the matches, then draw tonight's movie (must-watch picks get extra tickets) or settle it with a head-to-head showdown.

Built with AI

I used Claude (Anthropic) as my coding partner. I chose the idea, the direction and what to keep; the AI wrote the code and the tests. Along the way the automated tests caught real bugs, and I caught some myself while trying the app, like a scrollbar that flickered every time a card was swiped. The full log is in PROCESS.md.

The movie data

The 570 movies were hand-picked from around the world, including 20 Norwegian films. Titles, years, runtimes, directors and cast are public facts; the one-line pitches are written for this app. A script checks every line, and another cross-checks the facts against two open datasets (Wikipedia movie data and vega-datasets). That check found a real mistake, and it also showed that the reference data itself had errors, so every flag was reviewed by a person. No posters or images are used.

Privacy

No accounts, no tracking, no API keys. Names, settings, history and your "seen" list stay in your own browser. A share link contains the names and answers of the people in that game, so only send it to them.
